What happened

Turn this on and the Gen III+ unit loses the things that make it passive. It is the fastest way to see that the difference is real.

Reactors

Two reactors, the same bad day

On the left, a station that keeps its core cool with pumps, and needs electricity to run them. On the right, one that uses gravity, boiling and the outside air, and needs nothing at all. Pick an accident and watch both.

How to read it

  • Both buildings are cut open: the near quarter of the wall is taken off and every vessel is sliced down its own axis. Nothing has burst, that is just how you get to look in.
  • The fuel stands inside the reactor, in the water. Watch the level against the top of it. Covered, it stays grey. Uncovered, it glows, then melts.
  • The water in the pipes is solved, not animated. Each pipe runs at the speed continuity gives it, so the thin lines run fast and the fat ones run slow, and when the pump stops the loop keeps creeping at a twentieth of the speed.
  • Under the reactor: how much water is left, how hot it is, and how much heat the fuel is still making. That last number is the whole problem. Shutting a reactor down stops the chain reaction, not the heat.
  • Follow the steam out to the turbine. It turns the generator, and the megawatts beside it are what the station is putting on the grid. Scram the reactor and all of that stops.
  • Site shows the island from above: two stations, the town, the sea, and where anything that gets out would go. Inside opens the buildings up. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom, WASD to move; two fingers on a phone. The ⛶ button puts the view back.
  • Click any pipe to break it. The break is physics, not paint: the plant loses whatever that pipe was doing, and everything downstream follows. Cut the sea water line and the heat has nowhere to go; cut the pool's line on the passive unit and see what it still has left.
