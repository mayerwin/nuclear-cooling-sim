☢ Two ways to keep a reactor cool

A reactor that has been shut down still makes heat — about 6.5% of full power the instant the rods drop, and still ~0.5% a day later. For a 3,400 MW core that is 220 MW falling to 17 MW: enough to boil itself dry and melt in hours if nothing carries that heat away. Every nuclear accident in history is a story about losing that heat path.

Active cooling Gen II — Fukushima, TMI, most of today's fleet

  • Motor-driven pumps push water through the core.
  • Pumps need the grid → then diesels → then batteries.
  • Heat ends up in the sea or a river, via more pumps.
  • Operators must diagnose and act, often in minutes.
  • Chain of dependencies. Break any link and the clock starts.

Passive cooling Gen III+ — AP1000, ESBWR, SMRs

  • Water sits above the core and falls into it.
  • Hot water rises, cold water sinks — natural circulation.
  • Steam condenses on a steel shell cooled by an air draught.
  • Valves open when they lose power (fail-safe).
  • No pump, no diesel, no operator, for 72 hours.

How to read the diagram

  • Both plants are drawn as the same five boxes. Only one thing moves: where the spare water sits.
  • Orange is the reactor's own water, going red as the fuel overheats. Blue is water going in.
  • Moving dashes mean flow. A dark, still pipe has nothing in it.
  • Watch what happens when the power goes: on the left everything stops, and the spare water is at the bottom behind a pump. On the right the loop keeps moving and the water is already above the core.

What is real here, and what is not

Half the value of a simulation is knowing which numbers to trust. So:

ComputedWay–Wigner decay heat. Latent-heat boil-off against the net heat imbalance. Zr–H₂O oxidation above 1100 K with its 6.5 MJ/kg exotherm and 0.044 kg H₂ per kg of zircaloy. Saturated-pool containment pressure, p = pair(T) + psat(T) + pH2. Hydrogen migration and deflagration. Released Cs-137 integrated over time. Every flow animated in the diagram — if dashes are moving, the model is moving that water.
ScaledTime: the AUTO clock compresses quiet hours up to 1800× and drops to near real time when a core is coming apart. The diagram is a schematic, not a scale drawing: five boxes and the pipes between them. It shows what is connected to what and what is moving, not how big anything is or where it sits in the building — the site view is where the plant has a shape. Distance: one map tile is about 25 m, so the contamination footprint is a schematic of the plume, not a dispersion calculation. The site is staged for the camera rather than laid out to a real plant plan.
AssumedBoth units are 3,400 MW thermal so the comparison is like-for-like. The Gen-II unit is a large dry PWR containment; a Mark-I BWR is a harder case. The Gen-III+ unit is AP1000-class: PRHR, CMTs, ADS, IRWST gravity injection and a steel shell cooled by air and an evaporating film. 8 h of battery against a 72 h gravity tank.
FakedThe consequence figures (land, evacuees, cost, latent cancers) are scaling laws fitted to two data points — Fukushima ≈15 PBq and Chernobyl ≈85 PBq. They are the right order of magnitude and nothing more. The latent-cancer number is a straight LNT extrapolation, shown because people ask for it, not because the model believes it precisely. The tsunami inundation is cinematic, not hydrodynamic.
