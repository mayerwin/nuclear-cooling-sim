Where the site view's 79 ms went (Intel, 1829x1008): the panels' backdrop
blur 35 ms; the sorted Canvas-2D pass 21 ms (props 13, the two stations 9);
the ocean 6 ms; the vignette fill 2 ms. None of it scaled with pixel
density: it is per-path overhead, not fill. The pass is now drawn into a
layer and reused until the camera or the plant changes; the blur is gone;
the vignette is a CSS overlay.

On the engine: the same page on the RTX runs at 6 ms inside. Three.js is not
the limit. Chrome runs this page on the Intel GPU because Windows' per-app
graphics preference for Chrome is the default; the RTX figure above was
taken by giving a separate test browser binary a "high performance"
preference for the duration of the measurement and removing it after. To
have Chrome itself use the RTX: Windows Settings > System > Display >
Graphics > add chrome.exe > High performance.
